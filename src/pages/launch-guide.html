Stere Launch guide

Taking the new site live

A plain-English guide to replacing the current Webflow site with the new one. No coding knowledge assumed. Budget about half a day, plus a day of waiting for the domain to switch over.

Read this first. What we designed together is a complete, working set of pages — but it is a design, not yet a hosted website. Publishing it means putting the files on a web host and pointing stere.io at them. That is what this guide walks through.

Step 0 · Choose your route

There are three sensible ways to go live. Pick one before doing anything else.

A

Publish the design as-is on a simple host — recommended to start

Fastest and cheapest. You can be live this week. Best when you want the new site up now and will refine later. Downside: to change wording you come back to this project and re-publish, rather than editing in a CMS.

B

Have a developer rebuild it properly

Best long-term: faster pages, better SEO, a real content system. Takes one to three weeks of developer time. Use the pages here as the exact specification.

C

Rebuild it inside Webflow

Keeps your existing Webflow subscription, editing workflow and domain setup. Someone comfortable in Webflow re-creates these pages there. No DNS changes needed at all.

The rest of this guide covers Route A, since it is the one you can do yourself. If you choose B or C, skip to “Handing this to someone else” near the end.

Step 1 · Fix the things that only matter once real people visit

Three things on the site look finished but are not yet connected to anything. Sort these before launch, not after.

1. The forms don’t send anywhere yet

The Contact form and the Request-a-demo form show a “thank you” message, but nothing reaches your inbox. Sign up for a free form service — Formspree and Basin are the easiest — and they will give you a short web address to paste in. Ask me to wire it up and I’ll do it in a few minutes.

Your Google Calendar demo booking link already works and needs nothing.

2. There is no analytics yet

If you want to see visitor numbers, create a Google Analytics or Plausible account, copy the tracking snippet they give you, and send it to me to add to every page.

3. A few links are still placeholders

The Insights articles link to the Insights page rather than to real articles, and the Service Status link points at a status page that may not exist yet. Decide whether to write those articles, hide the section, or point the links elsewhere.

Step 2 · Get the website files

  1. Ask me to export the site as standalone files. I’ll produce one self-contained file per page — home, platform, each solutions page, about, insights, contact, demo, and the four legal pages — with all images and styling baked in.
  2. I’ll rename them to normal web addresses — index.html for the home page, platform.html, mgas.html and so on — so your URLs look clean.
  3. Download the whole set as a single ZIP folder and save it somewhere you’ll find again. Do not unzip it yet.

Step 3 · Put the files on a host

We’ll use Netlify. It is free for a site like this, and publishing is literally dragging a folder onto a web page.

  1. Go to netlify.com and click Sign up. Use your work email or a Google account. Choose the free plan.
  2. Once you’re signed in you’ll see a dashboard with a large dashed box that says something like “Drag and drop your site output folder here”.
  3. Unzip the folder from Step 2, then drag the unzipped folder into that box. Wait about thirty seconds.
  4. Netlify gives you a temporary address like gentle-otter-4821.netlify.app. Open it and click through every page. This is your new site, live on the internet — just not on your domain yet.
  5. Share that temporary link with colleagues for review. Nothing about stere.io has changed at this point, so there is zero risk.

Stop here until everyone is happy. Everything so far is reversible and invisible to the public. The next step is the one that changes what visitors see.

Step 4 · Before you touch the domain

Two safety measures. Both take five minutes and both save you from a bad afternoon.

  1. Back up the old site. In Webflow, use Export code if your plan allows, or simply take full-page screenshots of every page. If you ever need to go back, you’ll want a record.
  2. Photograph your current DNS settings. Log in to wherever your domain is registered (GoDaddy, Namecheap, Google Domains, Cloudflare — whoever you pay for stere.io). Find the page called DNS or DNS records and screenshot the entire list. This is your undo button.

Critical: do not delete your MX records. Those lines are what deliver your company email. You will be changing only the records named A, CNAME or ALIAS for @ and www. Leave every MX and TXT record exactly as it is.

Step 5 · Point stere.io at the new site

  1. In Netlify, open your site and go to Domain management → Add a domain. Type stere.io and confirm you own it.
  2. Netlify will show you exactly which DNS records to create — typically one A record for the bare domain and one CNAME for www. Keep this screen open.
  3. In your domain registrar’s DNS page, find the existing records pointing at Webflow (Webflow’s addresses usually start with 75.2. or end in .webflow.io). Edit those two records to the values Netlify gave you. Do not add duplicates — edit in place.
  4. Save. Then go back to Webflow and, under your project’s hosting settings, remove stere.io as a custom domain so the two systems don’t fight over it.
  5. Wait. The change usually takes fifteen minutes to two hours, occasionally up to a day. During the wait, some people may still see the old site — that is normal and temporary.
  6. Back in Netlify, check that the padlock — HTTPS / SSL certificate — shows as issued. It is automatic and free, but can take an hour after DNS resolves. If it hasn’t appeared after a few hours, click Renew certificate.

Step 6 · Launch-day checks

Once stere.io shows the new site, walk this list:

  • The address bar shows a padlock and https://
  • Both stere.io and www.stere.io work
  • Every navigation link and every footer link opens the right page
  • The site looks right on your phone, not just your laptop
  • Submit the contact form yourself and confirm the message arrives
  • Book a test slot through the demo page and check it lands in your calendar
  • Send yourself an email at your company address to confirm mail still flows
  • If your old site had pages at addresses that no longer exist, ask me to set up redirects so old links don’t break

If something goes wrong

Nothing here is permanent. To go back to the Webflow site: re-add stere.io as a custom domain in Webflow, then restore the DNS records from the screenshot you took in Step 4. Within a couple of hours the old site is back.

Keep your Webflow subscription running for at least a month after launch. It is cheap insurance while the new site settles.

Handing this to someone else

If you go with Route B or C, this is what the person needs from you:

  • Access to this project, which contains every page as the definitive design

  • Login access to the domain registrar, and to Webflow if they are rebuilding there
  • A note that the legal pages — Terms of Use, Privacy Policy, Master Agreement, Information & Security — should be reviewed by your counsel before launch
